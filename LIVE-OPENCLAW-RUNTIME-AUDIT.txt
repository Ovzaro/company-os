## FlyingPawfect/Viktor incidents

- The source remains in the active run-sheet directory.
- The hourly watcher finds it again.

## Deduplication and lifecycle

Effective incident deduplication is absent. Naming the incident “repeat” or “contained” does not prevent recreation.

There is no durable lifecycle such as:

```text
new → acknowledged → contained → resolved → suppressed
```

Unresolved source records therefore remain permanently actionable.

## Alerts

- Camille has a Telegram account mapping and route.
- The daily August 19 sweep reports successful delivery.
- The hourly watch reports not delivered on its latest run, which may mean it returned `NO_REPLY` rather than an alert failure.
- Earlier evidence records configured and delivered security notifications.

Thus Camille alerts are structurally configured and at least some outbound messages have worked.

## Security conclusion

Camille is genuinely protecting the system through detection, containment evidence, and escalation. She is not remediating it.

The quarantine volume is a **design and problem-management issue that should be fixed before expansion**. Without deduplication and closure, genuine new incidents will be buried in repetitive residue.

# Q. Decisive answers

1. **Is OpenClaw alive and healthy right now?**  
   Alive and listening, but not healthy. It is degraded by crashes, stuck scheduler work, backup faults, and configuration drift.

2. **Which agents are genuinely working today?**  
   Nexus/main and Camille. Main cron jobs and Camille’s security jobs executed today.

3. **Which agents are installed but mostly shells?**  
   Azir, Ryze, Swain, and Twisted Fate. They have models, instructions, Telegram routes, and one test session each, but no production integrations.

4. **Is the Sales workforce still runnable?**  
   Historically yes; structurally partial; not verified today. It should be considered blocked for safe production use.

5. **Is Camille actually protecting the system?**  
   Yes, through active monitoring, evidence preservation, and alerts. She does not remediate or close incidents.

6. **Why are there hundreds of quarantine entries?**  
   Real historical integrity findings combined with copy-based quarantine, active source records that remain unchanged, hourly rescanning, and no incident deduplication or resolution lifecycle.

7. **Are Telegram connections actually live?**  
   Structurally yes. Camille has recent successful delivery evidence. Most other agents have only stale or test-era evidence; their current outbound health is unverified.

8. **Is Nexus correctly configured?**  
   No. Delegation is correctly wired to the six Sales/security agents, but its identity, workflow contracts, and Company OS deployment are inconsistent.

9. **Are Azir/Ryze/Swain/Twisted Fate functional or just installed?**  
   Installed and conversationally executable, but not functional as Portal production agents.

10. **What is the biggest live-runtime risk?**  
    Source-of-truth and permission drift: broadly capable agents are operating under inconsistent instructions, with CRM safety depending more on prose than enforceable boundaries.

11. **What should be preserved?**  
    The live gateway, delegation framework, audit/session history, run-sheet contracts, human approval gates, Camille’s evidence system, Shen/Viktor role separation, and Portal’s domain/event architecture.

12. **What should be repaired?**  
    Deployment synchronization, permissions, backups, cron locking, incident lifecycle, credential handling, Nexus identity, Sales certification, and Portal connectivity.

13. **What should be retired?**  
    Duplicate blacklist cron, stale `/Desktop/core` deployment practices, embedded TRNDY base blocks where no longer canonical, active July residue, and obsolete one-off recovery scripts after archival.

14. **How far are Portal, Company OS, and OpenClaw from one coherent platform?**  
    Architecturally they have useful pieces, but operationally they are still three separate systems. The missing layer is a controlled Company OS deployment pipeline plus a tenant-aware Portal-to-OpenClaw event/API integration. Until those exist, the workforce shown in Portal is largely representational rather than operational.
